## 1. Acne Vulgaris

### New Findings Summary

This scan window has been one of the most active in acne therapeutics in recent memory, with three landmark developments:

1. **FDA OTC Switch for Differin Epiduo Gel** — On May 22, 2026, the FDA approved Galderma's Rx-to-OTC switch for Differin Epiduo Acne Gel (adapalene 0.1% / benzoyl peroxide 2.5%), making it the first retinoid+BPO combination available over the counter in the US. Retail availability projected for summer 2026. This dramatically increases patient access to a first-line combination therapy.

2. **AAD 2026 Updated Acne Guidelines** — The American Academy of Dermatology released updated management guidelines with conditional recommendations for: topical clascoterone, salicylic acid, azelaic acid, oral minocycline, sarecycline, combined oral contraceptives, and spironolactone. Notably, spironolactone use has tripled in recent years with growing safety/efficacy acceptance, and clascoterone (the first topical androgen receptor antagonist) received explicit guideline endorsement.

3. **Novel Acne Pipeline Advances** — XYNGARI™ (formerly DMT310), a once-weekly topical from Spongilla platform technology, showed strong Phase 2b results for moderate-to-severe acne. SB204 (Spruce Biosciences) is advancing in Phase III. Biofrontera's aminolevulinic acid HCl gel completed Phase 2b for moderate-to-severe acne. These represent mechanistic diversity beyond retinoid/antibiotic approaches.

### Clinical Implications for SKINgenius

- **OTC Epiduo Access**: Update acne treatment pathways to include OTC adapalene/BPO as a first-line OTC option for ages 12+. This changes the access landscape significantly.
- **Spironolactone Acceptance**: With AAD guideline endorsement and tripled utilization, our hormone-associated acne recommendations should more prominently feature spironolactone (especially for adult female hormonal acne).
- **Clascoterone Endorsement**: Add clascoterone as a guideline-conditionally-recommended topical for patients seeking anti-androgen approach without systemic side effects.
- **Probiotics Nuance**: Update gut-skin guidance to reflect that oral probiotics as adjuncts to antibiotics show promise, but Lactobacillus alone does not significantly reduce lesion count. Strain-specific evidence remains key.
- **DMT310/XYNGARI Pipeline**: Monitor this once-weekly topical — if approved, it could represent a novel non-retinoid, non-antibiotic approach (mechanical exfoliation + anti-inflammatory from Spongilla).
- **PIH Co-Treatment**: The Lopez-Estebaranz review reinforces that acne treatment in skin of color must simultaneously address PIH with agents like thiamidol, cysteamine, or azelaic acid.

## 2. Hyperpigmentation (Melasma / PIH)

### New Findings Summary

The hyperpigmentation landscape is undergoing a significant paradigm shift away from hydroquinone (HQ) dependence toward a new generation of tyrosinase inhibitors and combination approaches:

1. **Thiamidol Evidence Consolidation** — New data presented at PDE 2026 and published in multiple forums demonstrates thiamidol's 36.1% mMASI score reduction at 12 weeks in a 2026 randomized double-blind trial, with excellent tolerability across all skin types and no serious adverse events. Real-world case reports show success in stippled PIH from acne picking when applied 4×/day alongside retinoids and chemical peels. Thiamidol is solidifying as a first-line HQ alternative.

2. **RFM + Cysteamine Combination for Melasma** — A new study demonstrates that combining radiofrequency microneedling (RFM) with topical cysteamine cream significantly improves melasma severity, skin texture, and wrinkle reduction — especially with both in-office and at-home protocols. Cysteamine 5% cream also showed significant efficacy in a randomized double-blind trial of 50 melasma patients.

3. **Hydroquinone Safety Concerns & Alternatives** — Expert panels at PDE 2026 explicitly discussed HQ's safety concerns (ochronosis, irritation) and positioned alternatives like thiamidol, cysteamine, kojic acid, and lotus sprout extract as better-tolerated options providing "overall skin evening and glow" that patients desire. This represents a clear editorial shift in dermatology consensus.

4. **Cross-Condition PIH Treatment Framework** — Lopez-Estebaranz et al. published a comprehensive review in Am J Clin Dermatol (June 2026) on practical therapeutic strategies for acne-induced hyperpigmentation across all skin types — an important bridge between our acne and hyperpigmentation profiles.

## 3. Rosacea

### New Findings Summary

Rosacea research has seen a transformative 90 days with major therapeutic and mechanistic advances:

1. **DFD-29 (Emrosi™) FDA Approval** — The FDA approved DFD-29 (minocycline hydrochloride 40 mg extended-release capsules, branded Emrosi™ by Journey Medical) for the treatment of erythema and inflammatory lesions in adult rosacea patients. Phase 3 trials (MVOR-1 and MVOR-2) showed DFD-29 achieved 62.7% IGA success rate, significantly outperforming both doxycycline 40 mg (Oracea) and placebo. Inflammatory lesion reductions: 79.6% (MVOR-1) and 75.4% (MVOR-2), both statistically superior to doxycycline. This is the first FDA-approved oral rosacea treatment to outperform Oracea in head-to-head trials.

2. **Tranexamic Acid for Rosacea** — A groundbreaking new study (Xu et al.) shows oral tranexamic acid added to traditional rosacea therapies led to rapid improvements in papulopustular rosacea, with significant improvements in erythema, dryness, aesthetic improvement, and quality of life versus traditional therapy alone. This positions tranexamic acid as a crossover ingredient effective for both rosacea AND hyperpigmentation — a key SKINgenius differentiator.

3. **AD-Rosacea Comorbidity Review** — Zeng & Feng (2026) published a comprehensive review in Frontiers in Immunology establishing the association between atopic dermatitis and rosacea, from comorbidities to shared pathogenic mechanisms (innate immune dysregulation, inflammatory cytokines, abnormal blood vessel growth). This supports a systems-based approach to inflammatory skin conditions.

4. **Vitamin A/E Supplementation for Rosacea** — Longo et al. (2026) published in Dermatologic Therapy a study using reflectance confocal microscopy to document microscopic changes in rosacea during vitamin A and vitamin E supplementation therapy, providing imaging-verified evidence of disease modification through nutritional intervention.

5. **Rosacea-Systemic Inflammation Link** — New evidence strengthens the link between rosacea and systemic inflammatory diseases, including cardiovascular disease and inflammatory bowel disease, supporting the view that rosacea may be a manifestation of systemic inflammation rather than purely a skin condition.

6. **Prolonged Remission with Subantimicrobial Doxycycline** — A new study shows continued once-daily subantimicrobial-dose doxycycline (40 mg modified-release) significantly reduces relapse rate and inflammatory lesion counts in moderate-to-severe rosacea, supporting long-term maintenance therapy.

### Clinical Implications for SKINgenius

- **DFD-29/Emrosi™ Addition**: Add to rosacea treatment pathways as the new oral standard with superior IGA success and lesion reduction vs. doxycycline. This should be positioned as a first-line systemic option for moderate-to-severe papulopustular rosacea.
- **Tranexamic Acid as Rosacea Treatment**: This is a major crossover finding. Tranexamic acid is already in our hyperpigmentation profile — now it has evidence for rosacea erythema and papulopustular improvement. Update both condition profiles to reflect this dual-use ingredient.
- **AD-Rosacea Comorbidity**: Our rosacea profile should note the AD comorbidity connection. Patients with both conditions may benefit from systemic anti-inflammatory approaches rather than treating each in isolation.
- **Vitamin A/E Nutritional Intervention**: Add evidence-based nutritional supplementation (vitamin A + vitamin E) as a complementary approach for rosacea, backed by RCM imaging data.
- **Systemic Inflammation Framing**: Update rosacea profile to reflect the growing consensus that rosacea is a systemic inflammatory condition with cardiovascular and GI comorbidities, not just a cosmetic skin condition.
- **Skin of Color Recognition**: Update rosacea diagnostic guidance for skin of color — emphasizing that PIH is less common in rosacea vs. acne (folliculocentric vs. superficial inflammation), and erythema may be harder to detect on darker skin tones.

## Cross-Condition Highlights

### Shared Ingredient: Tranexamic Acid
This scan reveals **tranexamic acid** as the most significant crossover ingredient. Previously positioned primarily for melasma/hyperpigmentation, it now has emerging evidence for:
- **Melasma**: established efficacy (oral and topical)
- **PIH**: established adjunctive role
- **Rosacea**: new evidence for erythema and papulopustular improvement

**SKINgenius Implication**: Create a "tranexamic acid across conditions" knowledge module that can be referenced by multiple condition profiles.

### Shared Theme: Systems-Based Inflammation
All three conditions increasingly have evidence for systemic inflammatory underpinnings:
- **Acne**: gut-skin axis, microbiome disruption
- **Hyperpigmentation**: PIH as inflammatory sequelae requiring dual treatment
- **Rosacea**: cardiovascular, GI, neurological comorbidities; AD overlap

**SKINgenius Implication**: Our platform should emphasize holistic/systemic assessment rather than treating each condition in isolation.
