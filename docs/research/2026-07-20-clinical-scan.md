## 1. Acne Vulgaris

### 1.1 Updated Clinical Guidelines

**AAD Acne Vulgaris Guidelines (May 2026)**

- The American Academy of Dermatology released updated comprehensive guidelines in May 2026, based on a systematic review covering grading/classification, lab testing, and all treatment modalities.
- Key updates include:
  - **Topical retinoids remain first-line:** Tretinoin, adapalene, tazarotene, and trifarotene are all FDA-approved; no single retinoid demonstrates superiority over another.
  - **Combination therapy emphasized:** Topical retinoid + benzoyl peroxide (BPO) or antibiotic is recommended as initial therapy for most patients.
  - **Dietary interventions:** Growing evidence supports limiting high-glycemic-index foods and dairy (especially skim milk) as adjunctive measures.
  - **Complementary/alternative medicine:** Systematic review found insufficient evidence for most CAM modalities; tea tree oil has limited supportive data.
- **Citation:** AAD Acne Guideline Workgroup. "Guidelines of Care for the Management of Acne Vulgaris." _J Am Acad Dermatol_ (2026). [Dermatology Times coverage, May 28, 2026](https://www.dermatologytimes.com/view/aad-releases-updated-guidelines-for-the-management-of-acne-vulgaris)

### 1.2 Retinoid Protocol Advances

**Tretinoin Comprehensive Review (Nov 2025)**

- Narrative review (2000–July 2025) confirms robust RCT data for acne and photoaging; smaller RCTs show potential for melasma, PIH, striae, and as pre-treatment before chemical peels/laser.
- **Key takeaway for ingredient mapping:** Tretinoin should be mapped to acne, photoaging, melasma, and PIH with high confidence.

**Trifarotene and Newer Retinoid Formulations (2026)**

- AJMC review highlights newer formulations improving tolerability:
  - **Trifarotene 0.005% cream** — first retinoid specifically studied for both facial and truncal acne
  - **Tretinoin + BPO combination formulations** reduce irritation vs. separate application
  - **Encapsulated retinoids** (e.g., microsponge tretinoin) show improved tolerability with maintained efficacy
- **Citation:** "Topical Retinoids for Acne and Scarring: Newer Formulations Expand Multimodal Options." _AJMC_ (2026). [Link](https://www.ajmc.com/view/topical-retinoids-for-acne-and-scarring-newer-formulations-expand-multimodal-options)

**Isotretinoin + Antihistamines (Systematic Review, Mar 2026)**

- Systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD420251077931) evaluated combining oral isotretinoin with H₁-antihistamines.
- Findings: Combination reduces flushing and may improve tolerability of isotretinoin, but evidence quality is moderate; larger RCTs needed.
- **Citation:** Lyu M et al. "Oral isotretinoin combined with antihistaminics for acne vulgaris: systematic review, meta-analysis and trial sequential analysis." _Arch Dermatol Res_ (2026). [DOI:10.1007/s00403-025-04487-2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00403-025-04487-2)

**Topical Antibiotic Monotherapy — Insufficient (May 2026)**

- Network meta-analysis confirms topical antibiotic monotherapy provides only mild benefit for acne vulgaris. Reinforces that antibiotics should always be combined with BPO or retinoids, never used alone.
- **Citation:** Lyu M et al. "Topical Antibiotic Monotherapy Only Provides Mild Benefit for Acne Vulgaris." _Clin Drug Investig_ (2026). [DOI:10.1007/s40261-026-01553-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40261-026-01553-z)

### 1.3 Microbiome Research (Gut-Skin Axis)

**Oral Probiotics — Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis (Dec 2025)**

- Comprehensive search through Nov 2025 across PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov.
- **Finding:** Probiotics did NOT demonstrate significant reduction in acne lesion counts, but showed improvement in Global Acne Grading System (GAGS) scores.
- **Key strains studied:** _Lactobacillus_ species most commonly evaluated.
- **Caveat:** High heterogeneity across studies; GRADE certainty low-to-moderate.
- **Citation:** Tjiu JW, Lu CF. "Oral Probiotics in Acne Vulgaris: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Double-Blind Randomized Clinical Trials." _Medicina_ 61(12):2152 (2025). [PubMed 42153538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42153538/)

**Lactobacillus-Based Microbiome Therapy — GRADE Review (Mar 2026)**

- Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs specifically evaluating _Lactobacillus_-based interventions.
- Pooled lesion reductions: prebiotics 37.2%, probiotics 45.2%, postbiotics 49.5%.
- **Clinical implication:** Postbiotics (heat-killed bacterial lysates) show the most consistent signal; prebiotic/probiotic data remain heterogeneous.
- **Citation:** Abedin et al. "Lactobacillus-Based Microbiome Therapy for Acne Vulgaris: A GRADE Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." _J Cosmet Dermatol_ 25(3):e70792 (2026). [DOI:10.1111/jocd.70792](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jocd.70792)

**Gut-Skin Axis — Mendelian Randomization (Nov 2025)**

- MR study found _Bifidobacterium_ genera associated with reduced acne risk, while _Streptococcus_ genera linked to increased risk.
- Supports the gut-skin axis as a therapeutic target but confirms causality remains unproven.
- **Citation:** "Unravelling the causal link between gut microbiota and acne risk." _Skin Health Dis_ 5(6):448 (2025). [DOI](https://academic.oup.com/skinhd/article/5/6/448/8315044)

**Novel Non-Pharmacological Approaches (Jan 2026)**

- Narrative review on microbiome modulation strategies for acne: photodynamic therapy, light-based treatments, dietary interventions, and microbiome-preserving topical approaches.
- **Citation:** "Update on novel acne treatments: microbiome modulation and non-pharmacological approaches." _J Cosmet Dermatol_ (2026). [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0365059625001916)

### 1.4 Emerging Systemic Therapies

**Beyond Isotretinoin — Emerging Alternatives (May 2026)**

- Review identifies narrow-spectrum antibiotics, hormonal agents (anti-androgens, combined oral contraceptives), and immunomodulators as emerging personalized alternatives to isotretinoin.
- **Citation:** "Beyond Isotretinoin: New Review Explores the Evolving Landscape of Systemic Acne Therapy." _Dermatology Times_ (May 2026).

### 1.5 FDA Approvals & Pipeline (Acne)

- **No new FDA-approved acne-specific drugs in 2025–2026.** Trifarotene (0.005% cream) was FDA-approved in 2019 and remains the most recently approved topical retinoid for acne.
- Pipeline: Several microbiome-based topical therapies in phase II/III trials but none yet approved.

## 2. Hyperpigmentation

### 2.1 Novel Brightening Agents

**Thiamidol (Isobutylamido Thiazolyl Resorcinol)**

- Novel potent tyrosinase inhibitor developed by Beiersdorf; now with accumulating clinical data.
- 2025 clinical data (Music City SCALE conference): Demonstrated efficacy for melasma, solar lentigines, and PIH.
- 2025 JDD study: Clinical evaluation of thiamidol-containing formulations for facial hyperpigmentation showed statistically significant improvement in hyperpigmentation scores.
- **Safety profile:** Well-tolerated with minimal irritation; suitable for long-term OTC use.
- **Citation:** "Clinical Evaluation of Thiamidol-Containing Formulations for the Visual Management of Facial Hyperpigmentation." _J Drugs Dermatol_ (2025). [PubMed 41329151](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41329151/)

**2-MNG (2-Methylnicotinate Glucoside)**

- Novel depigmenting agent with emerging clinical evidence.
- 2026 RCT: 2-MNG-containing serum produced 15.8% decrease in Post-Acne Hyperpigmentation Index after 84 days.
- Head-to-head trial (Passeron 2026): 2-MNG-containing routine vs. thiamidol-containing routine for acne-induced PIH — comparative data pending full publication.
- **Citation:** Passeron et al. "Treatment of Acne-Induced Facial PIH With a New 2-MNG-Containing Routine Versus a Thiamidol-Containing Routine." _J Cosmet Dermatol_ 25(6):e70913 (2026). [DOI:10.1111/jocd.70913](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jocd.70913)

**Cysteamine 5% Cream**

- 2026 Cureus review on PIH in skin of color reports cysteamine 5% cream reduces hyperpigmentation indices within 16 weeks.
- Comparable efficacy to TXA mesotherapy in head-to-head study, with different safety profiles.
- **Citation:** "Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation in Skin of Color: Emerging Therapies and Treatment Algorithms." _Cureus_ (Apr 2026).

**Potassium 4-Methoxysalicylate (4MSK)**

- 2025 clinical data (Shirasugi et al., _J Cosmet Dermatol_ 24(3):e70112): Confirms skin-lightening effect via dual action on melanocytes and keratinocytes.
- **Citation:** Shirasugi Y et al. _J Cosmet Dermatol_ 24(3):e70112 (2025).

**Oligopeptide-34**

- 2025 Johns Hopkins study: 0.025% tretinoin nightly + morning oligopeptide-34 achieved 52% MASI reduction vs. 38% with peptide alone.
- Promising combination partner for retinoids.
- **Citation:** RealPeptides review (Apr 2026), referencing Johns Hopkins 2025 study.

### 2.2 Combination Therapy Advances

**Niosomal TXA 2%/Niacinamide 2% vs. HQ 4% (Nov 2025)**

- Landmark RCT: Niosomal formulation of TXA 2% + niacinamide 2% was comparable to hydroquinone 4% in melasma patients.
- Conventional (non-niosomal) TXA 5% + niacinamide 4% was also effective but with more irritation.
- **Clinical implication:** Niosomal delivery dramatically improves efficacy at lower concentrations — key for ingredient mapping.
- **Citation:** Ghasemiyeh P et al. "Safety and efficacy of niosomal and conventional TXA/niacinamide vs. HQ creams in melasma." _Sci Rep_ 15:42739 (2025). [DOI:10.1038/s41598-025-26693-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-26693-8)

**Tranexamic Acid — Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis (Jan 2026)**

- Comprehensive review of TXA for melasma and PIH covering topical, oral, and injectable routes.
- Oral TXA (250mg 2×/day typical) shows consistent MASI improvement; NOT associated with thromboembolism in multicenter propensity score-matched cohort (JAAD Int 2026).
- **Citation:** Scabine Pessotti et al. "The Use of Tranexamic Acid to Treat Melasma: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis." _Dermatol Ther_ (2026). [DOI:10.1155/dth/6691762](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/dth/6691762)

**Cysteamine vs. TXA Mesotherapy (May 2026)**

- Head-to-head: Cysteamine cream and TXA mesotherapy showed similar efficacy (48% improvement in TXA group at third visit; cysteamine comparable).
- Safety profiles differ: cysteamine has more local irritation risk; TXA mesotherapy has injection-related discomfort.
- **Citation:** _Dermatology Times_ (May 2026). [Link](https://www.dermatologytimes.com/view/cysteamine-cream-tranexamic-acid-mesotherapy-reveal-similar-efficacy-different-safety)

**Microneedling + Chemical Peel (Jun 2026)**

- 31-patient study: Pigment-correcting chemical peel + microneedling was more effective than microneedling alone for reducing erythema and improving brightness in hyperpigmentation.
- **Citation:** Chilukuri S. "Combination Approach to Hyperpigmentation." _JCAD_ (Jun 2026).

### 2.3 Hydroquinone — Regulatory & Safety Updates

**JDD Review (Apr 2026)**

- Comprehensive review of hydroquinone's clinical use, safety profile, and shifting regulatory landscape.
- Key points:
  - HQ remains gold-standard for short-term melasma treatment but long-term safety concerns persist (ochronosis risk with chronic use).
  - EU ban on OTC HQ (since 2001) continues; US FDA has not finalized its proposed HQ rule.
  - Growing momentum toward HQ-free alternatives in clinical practice, especially for maintenance therapy.
- **Citation:** "Hydroquinone and Emerging Depigmenting Agents: Clinical Guidelines, Regulatory History, and Global Safety Considerations." _J Drugs Dermatol_ (Apr 2026). [JDDonline](https://jddonline.com/hydroquinone-and-emerging-depigmenting-agents-clinical-guidelines-regulatory-history-and-global-safety-considerations/)

**Innovative Skin Depigmenting Strategies Review (Feb 2026)**

- MDPI review concludes most novel depigmenting agents remain at preclinical or early clinical stage; clinical evidence confined to small, short-term studies.
- **Citation:** "Innovative Skin Depigmenting Strategies: A Review." _Appl Sci_ 16(4):1735 (2026). [DOI:10.3390/app16041735](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/4/1735)

### 2.4 FDA Approvals & Pipeline (Hyperpigmentation)

- **No new FDA-approved hyperpigmentation drugs in 2025–2026.** Thiamidol is available OTC (Beiersdorf/Eucerin) but has not undergone the FDA NDA process.
- **Pipeline:** Several niosomal and liposomal formulations of TXA, niacinamide, and cysteamine in phase II trials globally.

## 3. Rosacea

### 3.1 Subtype Classification Update

**Phenotype-Based Classification — Now Standard (2025–2026)**

- Rosacea is now classified by **phenotype** rather than traditional subtypes (erythematotelangiectatic, papulopustular, phymatous, ocular).
- Two diagnostic phenotypes:
  1. **Persistent centrofacial erythema** (diagnostic alone)
  2. **Phymatous changes** (diagnostic alone)
- Major phenotypes: flushing, papules/pustules, telangiectasia, ocular manifestations, burning/stinging
- Minor phenotypes: dryness, edema, peripheral location
- **Clinical implication for SKINgenius:** Our condition mapping should use phenotype descriptors rather than the old subtype system.
- **Citation:** "Rosacea: an update on classification, mechanisms and management." _Medicine Today_ (Apr 2026). [Link](https://medicinetoday.com.au/mt/2026/april/feature-article/rosacea-update-classification-mechanisms-and-management)

**Exposome-Informed Framework (Oct 2025)**

- JEADV comprehensive review introduces exposome-informed rosacea management:
  - UV and pollution as key exposomal triggers
  - Barrier-repairing moisturizers with ceramides + niacinamide recommended
  - Topical antioxidants (vitamin C) counteract oxidative stress
  - Probiotics under investigation for rosacea (limited data)
  - Low-dose oral isotretinoin for non-responders to classical regimens
  - Demodex-associated rosacea: topical permethrin, oral ivermectin
  - Hormone-sensitive rosacea (perimenopausal women): hormone modulation may help
- **Citation:** "Exposome relevant rosacea therapies." _JEADV_ (Oct 2025). [DOI:10.1111/jdv.70112](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jdv.70112)

### 3.2 Emerging Therapies — Neuro-Vascular-Immune Triad

**Frontiers Review (Jun 2026): Targeting the Neuro-Vascular-Immune Triad**

- Comprehensive review of novel therapeutic targets:
  - **GABA derivatives (gabapentin, pregabalin):**
    - High-dose gabapentin (300mg TID) significantly improved flushing and redness in refractory erythema (multicenter RCT, n=87)
    - Gabapentin non-inferior to carvedilol for ETR (RCT, n=315)
    - Pregabalin + duloxetine: case report of marked improvement in neurogenic rosacea
  - **Antidepressants (paroxetine, duloxetine):**
    - Paroxetine 25mg/day improved CEA scores, flushing, burning, and depression (multicenter RCT, n=112)
    - Duloxetine improved clinical symptoms and reduced Th17/Treg ratio (RCT, n=89)
  - **Anti-CGRP agents (erenumab, galcanezumab, fremanezumab, rimegepant):**
    - Erenumab 140mg SC q4weeks reduced moderate-to-extreme flushing days (open-label trial, n=30)
    - CGRP monoclonal antibodies improved papules, pustules, erythema, flushing, and ocular symptoms (case series, n=13)
    - Rimegepant: case report of clearing painful cystic acne lesions (single case, needs replication)
  - **Physical neuromodulation:**
    - Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS): RCT (n=72) showed decreased CEA, flushing severity, and improved anxiety/depression/sleep
    - Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS): limited data
  - **Botulinum toxin type A:** for refractory flushing
  - **β-blockers (carvedilol, propranolol):** for flushing/erythema
- **Caveat:** Most emerging therapies are small-sample, single-center studies with short follow-up. Large-scale RCTs needed.
- **Citation:** "Therapeutic progress in rosacea: targeting the neuro-vascular-immune triad." _Front Immunol_ (2026). [DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2026.1876564](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2026.1876564/full)

### 3.3 FDA-Approved Rosacea Treatments

**DFD-29 (Minocycline Hydrochloride Modified Release) — FDA Approved 2024, Launched 2025**

- First systemic treatment to address both erythema and inflammatory lesions of rosacea.
- Extended-release formulation: 40mg minocycline once daily.
- Addresses the previous gap where topical metronidazole/ivermectin handled inflammatory lesions but not persistent erythema, and brimonidine/oxymetazoline handled erythema but not lesions.
- **Clinical implication:** First oral option targeting the full rosacea phenotype.
- **Citation:** "DFD-29: The Journey to FDA Approval and a New Era in Rosacea Management." _Dermatology Times_ (2025).

**Azelaic Acid 15% Foam (Finacea) — New FDA Approval 2025**

- FDA approved 15% azelaic acid foam for inflammatory papules and pustules of mild-to-moderate rosacea.
- Adds a new vehicle option to existing 15% gel.
- **Citation:** Managed Healthcare Executive (2025).

### 3.4 Trigger Management Updates

**Longitudinal Management Framework (Dec 2025)**

- Global Steering Committee (GSC) validated framework for rosacea long-term management:
  - Emphasizes trigger identification and avoidance as foundational
  - Sun protection (SPF 30+ broad spectrum) remains mandatory
  - Dietary triggers: alcohol, spicy foods, hot beverages, cinnamaldehyde-containing foods
  - Stress management as adjunctive
  - Maintenance therapy after clearance to prevent relapse
- **Citation:** "Exploring New Dimensions in Longitudinal Rosacea Management." _Dermatol Ther_ (2025). [DOI:10.1007/s13555-025-01612-x](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13555-025-01612-x)

### 3.5 Adjunctive & Procedural Therapies

**2025 Year in Review — Rosacea (Jul 2026)**

- Emerging adjunctive therapies gaining evidence: PRP, mesotherapy, radiofrequency microneedling.
- Laser modalities remain gold-standard for telangiectasia (KTP, PDL, Nd:YAG, IPL).
- **Citation:** "Dermatology Times 2025 Year in Review: Rosacea." _Dermatology Times_ (Jul 2026).

### 3.6 Pipeline

**Anti-IL-17 (Secukinumab)**

- Investigated for severe papulopustular rosacea; phase II level data only.
- **Status:** Not FDA-approved for rosacea.
