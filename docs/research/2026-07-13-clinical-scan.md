## 1. ACNE VULGARIS

### 1.1 Major Guideline Updates

#### EuroGuiDerm 2026 Acne Guideline (Published March 2026)

The EuroGuiDerm 2026 acne guideline (Nast et al., _JEADV_ 2026, DOI: 10.1111/jdv.70331) is the most significant European update since 2016. **Ten key changes:**

| #   | Update                                             | Detail                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Evidence Level    |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| 1   | **Isotretinoin recommended earlier**               | Now "strongly recommended" for severe papulopustular/moderate nodular and severe nodular/conglobate acne — discourages defaulting to repeated antibiotic cycles                                               | Strong consensus  |
| 2   | **Systemic antibiotics capped at 3 months**        | First definitive 3-month maximum duration (100% consensus). Preferred: doxycycline/lymecycline over minocycline/tetracycline. Must combine with topical agent                                                 | Strong consensus  |
| 3   | **Azithromycin discouraged**                       | Removed from standard algorithm per EMA directive. Exception: pregnancy (last resort only)                                                                                                                    | EMA directive     |
| 4   | **Spironolactone added (first time)**              | Adjunctive systemic option for females with papulopustular, nodular, or conglobate acne, especially with PCOS/androgen excess. Start 50 mg/day → 100 mg/day. No lab monitoring needed in healthy patients <45 | Moderate evidence |
| 5   | **Trifarotene positioned for truncal acne**        | Medium-strength recommendation; first topical retinoid with robust truncal evidence. Adapalene still preferred as default retinoid                                                                            | Moderate evidence |
| 6   | **Clascoterone approved but not yet in algorithm** | EU marketing authorization October 2025 for adults and adolescents 12+. Not integrated into algorithm yet (pending clinical experience)                                                                       | FDA/EU approved   |

| 9   | **Pregnancy protocol clarified**                   | Updated pregnancy treatment guidance                                                                                                                                                                          | Consensus         |
| 10  | **Contraceptive generation matters**               | Newer generation oral contraceptives recognized for acne management                                                                                                                                           | Moderate evidence |

#### AAD 2024/2025 Updated Acne Guidelines

The American Academy of Dermatology released updated guidelines (2024, published _JAAD_ 2024; AAFP summary November 2025) with **18 evidence-based recommendations and 5 good practice statements:**

- **Strong recommendations** for topical benzoyl peroxide, retinoids, and/or antibiotics and their fixed-dose combinations
- **Strong recommendation** for oral doxycycline in moderate-severe cases
- Systemic antibiotics must be combined with BPO to prevent resistance
- FDA-approved ages 9+ for: BPO 2.5%/adapalene 1% gel, tretinoin 0.1%/BPO 3% cream, trifarotene 0.005% cream, dapsone 5% gel, minocycline 4% foam
- Intralesional corticosteroid injection recommended for rapid inflammation/pain relief in larger lesions

### 1.2 Emerging Treatments & Pipeline

#### Clascoterone (Winlevi®) — Topical Androgen Receptor Antagonist

- **Mechanism:** First topical anti-androgen for acne; blocks dihydrotestosterone (DHT) binding at the androgen receptor in sebaceous glands — independently of systemic androgen pathways
- **EU approval:** October 2025 (adults + adolescents 12+)
- **US approval:** August 2020 (FDA); now also showing Phase 3 results for androgenetic alopecia (5% formulation — Breezula®)
- **Clinical data:** Phase II acne trials showed significant improvement vs. vehicle. Systematic review/meta-analysis published December 2025 (ScienceDirect) confirms efficacy and safety
- **SKINgenius relevance:** ✅ Include in database — novel mechanism, OTC-to-Rx pathway emerging, suitable for hormone-influenced acne patients

#### Denifanstat (ASC40) — FASN Inhibitor

- First-in-class fatty acid synthase (FASN) inhibitor for acne
- NDA accepted by China NMPA; Phase 3 ongoing
- Novel mechanism targeting lipid synthesis in sebaceous glands
- **Status:** Pipeline — not yet approved; monitor

#### Acne mRNA Vaccine

- ClinicalTrials.gov lists a study evaluating safety, efficacy, and immunogenicity of an acne mRNA vaccine for mild acne
- **Status:** Early-stage; not clinically actionable yet

#### Aminolevulinic Acid (ALA) Gel — Ameluz® for Acne

- Biofrontera completed Phase 2b trial for moderate-severe acne vulgaris (August 2025)
- Topline data expected early 2026, with FDA submission planned later in 2026
- Photodynamic therapy approach; would expand beyond actinic keratosis indication

### 1.4 Safety Considerations

- **BPO Benzene Risk:** Addressed and dismissed by EuroGuiDerm 2026 — no clinical basis for discontinuation. Proper storage at room temperature still recommended
- **Antibiotic Stewardship:** 3-month cap is now a hard guideline recommendation. Always combine with BPO. Avoid monotherapy
- **Isotretinoin:** Pharmacokinetic differences between brands noted — avoid mid-treatment brand switching. iPledge/teratogenicity protocols remain unchanged
- **Clascoterone:** Minimal systemic absorption confirmed; no significant hormonal side effects in trials

## 2. HYPERPIGMENTATION / MELASMA

### 2.1 Key Clinical Developments

#### Thiamidol (Isobutylamido-Thiazolyl Resorcinol)

**The most significant development in hyperpigmentation treatment in 2025–2026.**

- **Mechanism:** Most potent inhibitor of human tyrosinase identified to date
- **New evidence (2025–2026):**
  - Lekhavat et al. (2026, _Dermatol Ther_): Randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled study of 0.2% Thiamidol cream showed significant mMASI improvement at all timepoints vs. vehicle
  - Sarkar et al. (2025, _Indian Dermatol Online J_): Demonstrated efficacy and safety in skin-of-color melasma patients
  - Systematic review (2023, updated 2025): Meaningful lightening in 12–24 weeks at 0.1–0.2% concentration, 2–4x daily
  - Real-world data: Performs well across skin types; fewer side effects than hydroquinone
- **SKINgenius relevance:** ✅ High-priority inclusion — OTC availability, excellent tolerability, strong evidence base, suitable for maintenance therapy

#### 2-MNG (Melasyl™)

- **Mechanism:** Inhibits melanin synthesis downstream without directly blocking tyrosinase — dual inhibition of eumelanin and pheomelanin
- **Key data (2025):** 0.5% 2-MNG found non-inferior to 4% hydroquinone with better tolerability
- **2026 update:** Similar performance to triple combination cream when paired with niacinamide
- **Increasingly formulated in sunscreens and moisturizers** for broader accessibility
- **SKINgenius relevance:** ✅ Include — novel mechanism, favorable safety, growing evidence

#### Malassezin

- **Mechanism:** Suppresses melanogenesis via aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) pathway
- **Key data (2026):** Small randomized controlled trial demonstrated efficacy comparable to hydroquinone over 12 weeks, with rapid response and minimal side effects
- **Status:** Early clinical evidence; promising but needs larger trials
- **SKINgenius relevance:** ⚡ Watch — emerging, not yet mainstream

#### Tranexamic Acid — Growing Evidence Base

- Systematic review and meta-analysis published (Pessotti et al., 2026, _Dermatol Ther_, Wiley): Confirms efficacy both oral and topical forms
- **Niosomal tranexamic acid/niacinamide** vs. hydroquinone: Randomized double-blind trial (2025, _Scientific Reports_): Niosomal formulation showed comparable efficacy to hydroquinone with better tolerability
- **Mechanism:** Inhibits melanin production and targets vascular factors (dual pathway)
- **SKINgenius relevance:** ✅ Include — multiple formulations available, strong adjunctive evidence

#### Niacinamide for Melasma

- Narrative review published (2026, _ResearchGate_): Summarizes mechanisms (anti-inflammatory, decreases melanosome transfer) and clinical evidence
- **4% niacinamide** comparable to 4% hydroquinone in landmark trial (2002, PMC3142702) — still referenced in 2026 reviews
- Best used in combination approaches rather than monotherapy for melasma

#### Nanodiamond-Zinc Oxide (ND-ZnO)

- Novel topical agent for hyperpigmentation management
- 2026 case series (11 patients + 32 user outcomes) in _JDD_ — shows tolerability and improvement
- **Status:** Very early; case-series evidence only
- **SKINgenius relevance:** ❌ Skip for now — insufficient evidence

#### Cysteamine

- Antioxidant that reduces tyrosinase activity and inhibits melanogenesis
- Meta-analyses support efficacy and overall safety
- 2023 consensus highlights good patient acceptance despite distinctive odor
- **SKINgenius relevance:** ✅ Include — established option, well-tolerated, practical odor concern

### 2.2 Hydroquinone — Current Positioning

- Remains gold-standard for melasma treatment but with increasing caution
- Rebound pigmentation, ochronosis risk with long-term use, and irritation concerns
- Newer agents (thiamidol, 2-MNG, cysteamine) increasingly positioned as hydroquinone alternatives or maintenance therapy
- Triple combination cream (HQ + tretinoin + corticosteroid) still has strongest evidence but requires careful monitoring

### 2.4 Safety Considerations

- **Hydroquinone:** Risk of exogenous ochronosis with prolonged use, especially in darker skin types (Fitzpatrick IV-VI). Limit to 3–6 month courses with dermatologist supervision
- **Tranexamic Acid (oral):** Contraindicated in patients with history of thromboembolic disease. Topical formulation is safer alternative
- **Thiamidol:** Excellent tolerability profile across skin types. No significant irritation in clinical trials
- **2-MNG:** Better tolerated than HQ in head-to-head comparison; suitable for long-term maintenance
- **Sun protection:** Remains foundational — all topical treatments must be paired with broad-spectrum SPF 30+ (mineral filters preferred for sensitive skin)

## 3. ROSACEA

### 3.1 Major Guideline & Classification Updates

#### ROSCO Consensus & Phenotype-Based Approach

- The global ROSacea COnsensus (ROSCO) panel continues to shift treatment from a subtype-based to **phenotype-based approach** (Tan et al. 2017; Schaller et al. 2017, 2019)
- Treatment matched to predominant features (erythema, papulopustules, phyma, ocular) rather than "subtype"
- This approach is now reflected in the Canadian Clinical Practice Guidelines for Rosacea (CCPGR)

#### Longitudinal Management Framework (Schaller et al. 2025)

- Published in _Dermatology and Therapy_ (December 2025): New framework for longitudinal rosacea management
- Emphasizes trigger management, barrier repair, and maintenance strategies alongside prescription treatment
- Highlights lack of evidence for rarer subtypes (phymatous, granulomatous)

#### UK NHS Rosacea Guidelines V1.0 (June 2026)

- New local formulary guideline published by NHS Notts APC
- References brimonidine tartrate gel for facial erythema
- Provides treatment algorithm aligned with NICE evidence summaries

### 3.2 New FDA Approvals & Pipeline

#### Emrosi™ (DFD-29) — FDA Approved June 2026 🆕

**The most significant rosacea drug approval in 2026.**

- **What:** Low-dose modified-release minocycline hydrochloride (40 mg) oral capsule
- **FDA Approval:** November 2024 (with market launch 2025); additional reporting June 2026
- **First oral systemic therapy approved for BOTH erythema and inflammatory lesions** in rosacea
- **Phase 3 results (MVOR-1 and MVOR-2):**
  - 62.7% IGA success rate
  - Significantly greater lesion reduction than both doxycycline and placebo
  - Minimal adverse events (dyspepsia most common, small percentage)
  - No detectable effects on skin, GI, or vaginal microflora in 16-week microbiome study
- **SKINgenius relevance:** ✅ Include — game-changing for moderate-severe papulopustular rosacea with concurrent erythema

#### Minocycline 1.5% Foam (Zilxi®) — Topical

- FDA-approved for papulopustular rosacea (since 2020; more data 2025–2026)
- 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis (Alamri et al., _Frontiers in Medicine_ 2025): 5 RCTs, 2,453 participants — statistically significant reduction in inflammatory lesion count and IGA treatment success
- Good safety profile; no systemic antibiotic exposure concerns
- **SKINgenius relevance:** ✅ Include — valuable topical alternative for patients who want to avoid oral antibiotics

#### Benzoyl Peroxide 5% Cream (Epsolay®)

- FDA-approved April 2022 for papulopustular rosacea
- Encapsulated BPO formulation reduces irritation
- Growing use as adjunctive therapy
- Now included in rosacea treatment discussions alongside traditional agents

#### Ocular Rosacea

- Requires ophthalmology co-management
- Lid hygiene, warm compresses, artificial tears
- Oral doxycycline for moderate-severe cases
- Cyclosporine ophthalmic for inflammatory component

### 3.4 Trigger Research & Barrier Science (2025–2026)

- **TRPV1/TRPV4 receptor pathways** confirmed as key mechanistic drivers of neurogenic inflammation and flushing (Steinhoff et al.)
- **Trigger identification remains the single most impactful self-care action** — 4-week logging protocol recommended
- Most common triggers (>50% of patients): UV exposure, hot weather, exercise, alcohol (especially red wine), spicy food, emotional stress
- **Stress/sleep axis:** Well-documented influence on rosacea severity (Mar & Rivers 2023, PMID 37898903)
- **Barrier repair:** Ceramide-based, fragrance-free, low-surfactant cleansers confirmed as foundational care across all phenotypes (CCPGR, ROSCO)

### 3.6 Safety Considerations

- **Brimonidine rebound erythema:** Documented in subset of patients; counsel on risk
- **Oral minocycline (DFD-29):** Low-dose (40 mg MR) avoids typical antibiotic side effects. No detectable microbiome impact in 16-week study. Contraindications same as other tetracyclines (pregnancy, children <12)
- **Topical minocycline foam:** No systemic absorption concerns per pharmacokinetic data
- **Azelaic acid:** Generally well-tolerated; initial tingling/burning common and self-limiting
- **Ivermectin:** Excellent safety profile; rare facial burning
- **Sunscreen:** Mineral filters (zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) with iron oxides preferred — chemical filters can trigger flares in rosacea patients

## 4. CROSS-CUTTING OBSERVATIONS

### 4.1 Common Themes Across Conditions

1. **Combination therapy remains king** — No single agent is universally sufficient. Layered approaches with foundational skincare + targeted actives + sun protection outperform monotherapy
2. **Antibiotic stewardship intensifying** — EuroGuiDerm's 3-month hard cap for acne antibiotics reflects growing resistance concerns. Applies equally to rosacea (sub-antimicrobial doxycycline preferred)
3. **Novel mechanisms expanding options** — Clascoterone (androgen receptor), thiamidol (tyrosinase), DFD-29 (low-dose minocycline for dual indication), 2-MNG (downstream melanin inhibition) all represent genuinely new therapeutic approaches
4. **Barrier-first philosophy** — Every major guideline now explicitly lists barrier repair and gentle skincare as foundational before any prescription
5. **Phenotype-based treatment** — Moving away from one-size-fits-all toward matching treatment to predominant features (especially in rosacea, but increasingly in acne severity grading)
