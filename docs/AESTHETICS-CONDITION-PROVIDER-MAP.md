# Aesthetics Condition → Treatment → Provider Map

> **SKINgenius Clinical Reference** | Compiled: 2026-09-15
> Purpose: map common aesthetic skin concerns to treatments, providers, costs, recovery, and results duration. Intended as reference data for SKINgenius treatment-matching and provider-directory features.
> **Costs are 2025–2026 US national ranges** and vary by region, practice type, and injector experience. This is educational reference data, not medical advice.

## 1. Provider Tiers (Read First)

| Tier | Who | Typical Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Core physicians** | Board-certified dermatologist (ABDerm), plastic surgeon (ABPS), facial plastic surgeon (ABFPRS), oculoplastic surgeon (ASOPRS) | All injectables, lasers, deep peels, surgery (per specialty) |
| **Mid-level injectors** | NP / PA with aesthetic training; RN injectors in most states require physician delegation | Neurotoxin, HA fillers, biostimulators (with training); **no surgery**; supervision rules vary by state |
| **Medical estheticians / laser techs** | Licensed estheticians in medical settings, supervised | Superficial/medium peels, microneedling, IPL/energy devices where state law allows; **no needles, no prescriptions** |
| **Surgeons only** | ABPS plastic, ABFPRS facial plastic, ASOPRS oculoplastic (jaw work may involve OMFS) | Facelift, blepharoplasty, fat grafting, implants, genioplasty |

**Rule of thumb:** the deeper the needle or the more permanent the result, the higher the tier required.

## 3. Condition Deep Dives

### 3.1 Volume Loss / "Ozempic Face" (cheek + temple hollowing)

**What's happening:** deflation of deep facial fat pads (malar, submalar, SOOF, temporal pads) with concurrent skin-elasticity decline. Rapid GLP-1 weight loss accelerates it, but any significant weight loss produces the same pattern. Presents as cheek/temple hollowing, deepened nasolabial folds, skin that looks "loose without volume."

**Clinical sequencing note:** delay major correction until weight is stable ~3–6 months. Correcting during active loss means chasing a moving target.

| Treatment | Type | Provider | Cost | Recovery | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sculptra (PLLA) | Biostimulator | Derm/PS/NP/PA with training | $700–$1,200/vial; 1–3 vials/session × 2–3 sessions ($1,500–$4,500 typical total) | 2–4 days swelling | Visible 4–12 wks; ~2 yrs+ |
| Radiesse (CaHA) | Biostimulator | Derm/PS/NP/PA | $700–$1,100/syringe | 2–5 days | 12–18 months |
| HA filler — cheeks (Juvéderm Voluma XC, Restylane Lyft) | Filler | Derm/PS/NP/PA | $700–$1,500/syringe; 2–4 syringes typical | 3–7 days | 12–24 months |
| HA filler — temples | Filler | Experienced physician injector preferred (vascular zone) | $600–$1,200/syringe | 3–7 days | 12–18 months |
| Structural fat grafting | Surgical | Plastic/facial plastic surgeon | $3,000–$10,000+ | 1–2 weeks initial; months to settle | Surviving fat (30–70% take) is permanent |
| Facelift (if excess skin) | Surgical | Plastic/facial plastic surgeon | $15,000–$30,000+ total | 2–4 weeks | 7–10+ years |

**Best fit:** Sculptra is the standout for diffuse/global volume loss (see §4). HA for precise compartment deficits. Fat grafting when the patient wants permanence and accepts surgical recovery.

### 3.2 Fine Lines and Wrinkles (forehead, crow's feet, nasolabial folds)

**What's happening:** two distinct mechanisms. Dynamic lines come from repeated muscle folding (forehead, glabella, crow's feet) and respond to neurotoxin. Static lines and folds come from collagen/elastin loss and photodamage (nasolabial folds) and respond to volume, resurfacing, or surgery.

| Treatment | Type | Provider | Cost | Recovery | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neurotoxin (Botox Cosmetic, Dysport, Xeomin, Jeuveau, Daxxify) | Injection | MD/NP/PA | $10–$18/unit ($15–$25 premium cities); $300–$600/session; forehead+crows+glabella ≈ 40–60 units | None; onset 3–7 days | 3–4 months (Daxxify up to ~6) |
| HA filler — nasolabial folds | Filler | MD/NP/PA | $600–$1,000/syringe | 1–3 days | 6–18 months |
| Microneedling (± RF) | Energy | Med esthetician (supervised, state-dependent) / NP/PA / derm | $300–$700/session; RF $1,000–$1,500; series of 3 | 1–3 days | Progressive; maintenance q6–12 mo |
| Fractional laser (Fraxel Dual, non-ablative) | Laser | Derm / physician-directed | $1,000–$1,500/session | 3–5 days | 1–2 yrs, maintenance |
| Chemical peel (glycolic/TCA) | Peel | Esthetician (superficial) / physician (medium+) | $150–$500 superficial; $500–$1,000 medium | 3–7 days | Months; repeat as needed |
| Topicals: tretinoin, SPF | Rx/OTC | Dermatologist prescribes | $20–$150/mo | None | Ongoing while used |
| Brow/forehead lift | Surgical | Facial plastic / plastic surgeon | ASPS avg surgeon fee ≈ $5,460; total $4,600–$11,000 | 1–2 weeks | 7–10 years |
| Blepharoplasty (if upper-lid hooding drives the lines) | Surgical | Oculoplastic / facial plastic / plastic surgeon | ASPS avg surgeon fee $3,359 upper; total $4,000–$8,000+ | 1–2 weeks | 5–10+ years |
| Facelift (for NLF + jowl complex) | Surgical | Plastic/facial plastic surgeon | $15,000–$30,000+ total | 2–4 weeks | 7–10+ years |

**Best fit:** neurotoxin for dynamic lines, filler for static folds, retinoids forever as maintenance. Surgery when skin excess and fold depth exceed what injectables address.

### 3.3 Facial Asymmetry

**What's happening:** asymmetry has a cause — side-dominant masseter hypertrophy (chewing/bruxism), differential fat-pad volume, skeletal/dental base, or nerve injury (e.g., Bell's palsy). **Diagnose the cause before treating.** Dental and TMJ causes need dental referral first.

| Treatment | Type | Provider | Cost | Recovery | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neurotoxin to dominant masseter / uneven brow / asymmetric smile | Injection | MD/NP/PA | $200–$600/session | None | 3–4 months |
| HA filler to balance cheeks, chin, jaw, temples | Filler | MD/NP/PA | $600–$1,000/syringe | 1–3 days | 6–24 months |
| Sculptra for diffuse asymmetric deflation | Biostimulator | MD/NP/PA w/ training | $700–$1,200/vial | 2–4 days | ~2 years |
| Cheek / chin / jaw implants | Surgical | Facial plastic surgeon or OMFS | $3,000–$8,000+ per site | 1–2 weeks | Permanent |
| Genioplasty (chin repositioning) | Surgical | OMFS / plastic surgeon | $5,000–$12,000 | 2–4 weeks | Permanent |
| Fat grafting (differential volume) | Surgical | Plastic/facial plastic surgeon | $3,000–$10,000 | 1–2 weeks | Permanent (surviving fat) |

**Best fit:** toxin + filler for soft-tissue asymmetry; implants or orthognathic surgery for skeletal asymmetry. Fillers are reversible pilots for surgical decisions.

### 3.5 Under-Eye Hollows / Tear Troughs

**⚠️ High-risk, high-visibility zone.** Thin skin shows everything: swelling can persist for weeks, and poor technique (too superficial, too much product) produces visible Tyndall bluing or lumps that dissolve slowly or not cleanly. This is a "least experienced injector wins nothing" zone — steer users to physician-level injectors who treat this area routinely.

**Differential first:** dark circles can be pigmentation, vascularity, or shadow. Filler fixes shadow/volume only. A dermatologist should classify before anyone injects.

| Treatment | Type | Provider | Cost | Recovery | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low-G-prime HA via cannula (Restylane-Lyne/Silk/Refyne, Juvéderm Volbella, Belotero) | Filler | **Experienced physician injector strongly preferred** | $600–$1,200 total (0.5–1 syringe for both eyes) | 1–3 days bruising; swelling may last 2+ wks | 6–12 months (dissolves slowly here) |
| PRP / skin booster under-eye | Injection | Derm / trained mid-level | $400–$800/session | 1–3 days | 6–12 months |
| Lower blepharoplasty ± fat repositioning | Surgical | Oculoplastic (preferred), facial plastic, or plastic surgeon | ASPS avg surgeon fee $3,876; total $4,000–$8,000+ | 1–2 weeks; bruising 2–3 wks | Long-lasting; often 10+ years, frequently once-per-lifetime |
| Fat grafting / fat repositioning | Surgical | Oculoplastic / facial plastic surgeon | $3,000–$8,000 | 1–2 weeks | Permanent (surviving fat) |

**Best fit:** conservative cannula filler for early, mild hollows. Blepharoplasty with fat repositioning for significant hollows, festoons, or excess fat. Under-inject; you can add, removal is imperfect.

### 3.7 Skin Laxity (jowls, neck)

**What's happening:** elastin and collagen decline, retaining ligaments loosen, deep fat pads descend, and submental fat accumulates. Energy devices produce modest tightening in mild-to-moderate laxity; no device replaces surgery for true excess skin.

**Best fit:** energy devices + biostimulators for early laxity, surgery for the real thing. Say it plainly: jowls past a certain threshold are a surgical problem.

### 3.8 Acne Scars (rolling, boxcar, ice pick)

**Match treatment to scar type — this is the core clinical logic:**

| Scar type | Appearance | Best-matched treatments | Cost | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ice pick** | Narrow (<2 mm), deep pits | TCA-CROSS ($150–$500/session); punch excision ($150–$300/scar, physician) | Low per session; needs series | 5–7 days per spot |
| **Boxcar** | Round/oval, sharp vertical edges | Fractional CO₂/erbium laser ($1,000–$2,500/session); punch grafting | Mid | 5–7 days (CO₂), redness up to 4 wks |
| **Rolling** | Undulating, wave-like; fibrous tethering | Subcision ($500–$1,500/session, ± filler); RF microneedling ($1,000–$1,500/session) | Mid | 3–7 days |
| **All types** | — | Microneedling baseline series ($300–$700/session × 3–6, 24–48 h recovery) | Lowest | 24–48 h |

**Providers:** dermatologist-led for subcision, TCA-CROSS, punch techniques, and ablative lasers. Medical estheticians can run microneedling series and superficial peels under medical supervision in most states. **Bellafill** (PMMA collagen filler, FDA-approved for rolling acne scars): $800–$1,500/syringe, 5+ year duration, physician injector.

**Expectations:** combined-modality series over 6–12 months typically yields 25–75% improvement. Scar improvement is durable/permanent once achieved, but scars are not erased. **Treat active acne first** — scar work on inflamed skin fails.

### 3.9 Hyperpigmentation (sun spots, melasma)

**Classify before treating — these are different diseases.** Lentigines (sun spots) are localized pigment deposits that lasers/IPL remove well. Melasma is hormone/UV-driven, often has a dermal component, relapses, and **heat from IPL and many lasers makes it worse**. A dermatologist (Wood's lamp exam) should classify before any light-based treatment.

#### Sun spots (lentigines, photodamage)

| Treatment | Provider | Cost | Recovery | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Topicals: hydroquinone 2–4%, azelaic acid, retinoid, vitamin C | Dermatologist (Rx) / OTC | $10–$150 | None | While used; spots fade over 2–6 months |
| IPL / BBL photofacial | Physician-directed (state rules for operators) | $300–$600/session × 3–5 | 0–3 days | Lesion removal permanent; new spots form with UV |
| Chemical peel (glycolic/TCA superficial) | Med esthetician / mid-level | $150–$500/session × 3–6 | 3–7 days | Months; maintenance |
| Pico laser (stubborn pigment; safer in darker skin) | Dermatologist | $400–$800/session | 0–3 days | Durable; maintenance |
| Cryotherapy (isolated spots) | Physician | $50–$150/spot | 7–10 days healing | Permanent for treated lesion |

**Caution:** IPL in Fitzpatrick IV–VI skin types carries burn/post-inflammatory pigment risk; use a provider experienced with darker skin.

#### Melasma (management, not cure)

| Treatment | Provider | Cost | Recovery | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Triple cream (e.g., Tri-Luma: hydroquinone 4% + tretinoin + mild steroid) | Dermatologist Rx | $50–$150/8–12 wk cycle | None | First line. Cycle HQ; avoid >6 months continuous (ochronosis risk) |
| Azelaic acid 15–20%, cysteamine, tranexamic acid | Derm / OTC varies | $25–$150 | None | Alternatives/maintenance between HQ cycles |

| **IPL / heat lasers** | — | — | — | **Avoid in true melasma — heat triggers melanocyte rebound** |
| Sun protection (tinted mineral SPF with iron oxides + visible-light protection) | Everyone | $20–$60 | — | The actual long-term treatment; visible light drives melasma |

## 4. Biostimulators Deep Dive

### What they are

Biostimulatory injectables do not fill; they recruit the body to make collagen. Injected material acts as a scaffold that triggers fibroblast-driven collagenesis over weeks to months.

| | **Sculptra** | **Radiesse** |
|---|---|---|
| Active material | Poly-L-lactic acid (PLLA), 367.5 mg/vial, reconstituted with sterile water | Calcium hydroxylapatite (CaHA) microspheres in gel carrier |
| Mechanism | Gradual collagen neogenesis over 4–12 weeks | Immediate volume (gel) + collagen stimulation behind it |
| Protocol | 2–3 sessions spaced 4–8 weeks; deep dermis/supraperiosteal depot technique | 1–2 syringes/session; dilute ("hyperdilute") use for skin quality, neck, hands |
| Onset | 4–12 weeks | Immediate + progressive |
| Duration | ~24 months (often cited 2–5 years) | 12–18 months |
| FDA indications | HIV facial lipoatrophy; nasolabial wrinkles. Off-label widespread for global facial volume | Nasolabial folds, midface volume, dorsal hands |
| Not for | Lips, tear troughs, nose, precision contour | Lips, tear troughs, nose |
| Cost | $700–$1,200/vial; typical face $1,500–$4,500 | $700–$1,100/syringe |
| Aftercare | 5-5-5 massage: 5 minutes, 5×/day, 5 days | Similar massage protocol |

### How they differ from hyaluronic acid fillers

| Dimension | HA filler (Juvéderm, Restylane) | Biostimulator (Sculptra, Radiesse) |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Physical volume (G-prime/cohesivity) | Gradual collagen neogenesis |
| Onset | Immediate | Weeks (Sculptra), immediate+progressive (Radiesse) |
| **Reversibility** | **Dissolvable with hyaluronidase in 24–48 h** | **Not reversible** |
| Precision | High — lips, tear trough, nose, single fold | Low — global diffuse areas only |
| Best for | Precision structural contour, first-time patients, trial corrections | Global volume loss, "Ozempic face," temples, lateral face, skin quality, lower cost per year of effect |
| Cost logic | ~$2,000+/yr at 1–3 syringes annually | $1,500–$4,500 lasting ~2 yrs ≈ cheaper per year |
| Key complications | Vascular occlusion (rare), Tyndall effect, nodules — mostly treatable | Papules/nodules/granulomas — **harder to treat**, prevention through technique |
| Failure mode | Overfill, migration | Too-superficial injection → visible/palpable papules |

### Best use cases

- Global facial volume loss — the defining indication, especially post-weight-loss deflation
- Temporal hollowing, lateral cheek/lateral face
- Skin quality (diluted Radiesse, Sculptra) — texture and crepey skin
- Patients who want fewer maintenance visits per year
- **Not** precision areas: lips, tear troughs, nose, jawline edge definition where millimeter control matters

### Provider qualifications required

- Physician (dermatologist, plastic surgeon, facial plastic surgeon) **or** NP/PA with documented biostimulator-specific training
- Injection depth discipline: deep dermis/supraperiosteal. Superficial placement causes papules
- Dilution/reconstitution competence: dilution ratio, hydration time (Sculptra), and dilute-Radiesse technique are technique-defining
- Post-injection massage protocol education for the patient
- Red flag for users: a provider who quotes Sculptra as "just a long-lasting filler" does not understand the product. It is a collagen program, not a filler.

## 5. Safety Notes & Red Flags

- **Vascular occlusion risk zones:** glabella, nose, tear troughs. Retrograde injection into ophthalmic circulation causes rare but devastating blindness. Only trained injectors, with hyaluronidase on-site for HA.
- **IPL on melasma is a common mistake** — heat triggers rebound pigment. Classify pigment first.
- **Hydroquinone** beyond ~6 months continuous raises ochronosis risk; cycle it.
- **GLP-1 patients:** wait for weight stability (~3–6 months) before major volume correction.
- **Board certification verification:** abps.org (plastic), abfprs.org (facial plastic), aad.org (dermatology), asoprs.org (oculoplastic). "Cosmetic surgeon" is not a board-certified specialty — verify the underlying board.
- **Pricing that is far below market** usually means diluted product, unqualified injection, or gray-market vials.
