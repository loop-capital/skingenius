## Category 2: CIR / INCI / Ingredient Safety Data

### Finding 1 — EU Formaldehyde-Releasing Preservatives: New Warning Rule (Effective Now)

- **Source:** https://en.reach24h.com/news/insights/cosmetic/eu-cosmetic-regulatory-updates-2026
- **Title:** 2026 EU Cosmetic Regulatory Updates: What Brands Must Comply With
- **Relevance:** HIGH
- **Summary:** As of 2026, products containing authorized preservatives that release formaldehyde must carry a mandatory warning "releases formaldehyde" if the total formaldehyde concentration exceeds 0.001% (10 ppm). Companies using o-Phenylphenol, Sodium o-Phenylphenate, or formaldehyde-releasing preservatives must reassess formulation compliance against updated Annex requirements. The prohibition of o-Phenylphenol in applications with inhalation exposure is new — spray, mist, and aerosol formats require immediate reformulation assessment.
- **Product relevance:** SKINgenius recommendation engine flags products with formaldehyde-releasing preservatives (e.g., DMDM Hydantoin, Quaternium-15, Imidazolidinyl Urea). This new EU labeling requirement affects any product recommendations for EU users and signals tightening global scrutiny.
- **Action needed:** Add "releases formaldehyde" as a new ingredient flag in our safety scoring system. Cross-reference our 236 seeded products for formaldehyde-releasing preservatives and flag concentrations above 0.001% threshold.
- **Sources:** Reach24H (Aug 27, 2026), Safic-Alcan (Aug 31, 2026) — **2+ independent sources confirmed**

### Finding 2 — EU C9–C21 PFAS Ban (September 2026)

- **Source:** https://www.cirs-group.com/en/news/industry-news
- **Title:** EU Adopts Delegated Regulation Adding Long-Chain PFAS to Restricted Substances
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM
- **Summary:** On September 7, 2026, the EU adopted a Delegated Regulation adding C9–C21 long-chain perfluorocarboxylic acids, their salts, and related compounds to restricted substances. These PFAS compounds have been used in some cosmetic formulations for water and stain resistance.
- **Product relevance:** While not common in typical skincare, PFAS can appear in long-wear makeup, waterproof sunscreens, and some barrier creams. SKINgenius should flag any products containing PFAS-derived ingredients.
- **Action needed:** Add PFAS-related ingredients to our "watch list" in the ingredient taxonomy. Check if any of our 108 seeded ingredients or 236 products contain perfluoro- or polyfluoro- compounds.
- **Sources:** CIRS Group (Sep 11, 2026) — **Single source; flag as needing confirmation**

### Finding 3 — CIR Expert Panel: 126 Acrylates Copolymers Safety Confirmed

- **Source:** https://skinsort.com/products/seonn/pdrn-pink-peptides-regenerating-face-serum (and secondary skinsort.com reference)
- **Title:** CIR Safety Assessment — Acrylates Copolymers
- **Relevance:** LOW-MEDIUM
- **Summary:** The CIR Expert Panel assessed the safety of 126 acrylates copolymers and concluded they are safe in cosmetics at current use levels when formulated to be non-irritating. These are film-forming agents used in leave-on products for texture and wear properties.
- **Product relevance:** Acrylates copolymers appear in some sunscreens, primers, and long-wear skincare. Not a primary concern for SKINgenius's core recommendation engine but relevant for completeness.
- **Action needed:** None urgent. Note for future ingredient taxonomy expansion.
- **Sources:** SkinSort (Aug 17 & Sep 7, 2026) — **Single source type; flag as needing CIR primary document**

### Finding 4 — PEG Castor Oils: CIR Confirms Safety (with Caveats)

- **Source:** https://hbmag.com/what-is-peg-40-hydrogenated-castor-oil-and-is-it-safe/
- **Title:** What Is PEG 40 Hydrogenated Castor Oil And Is It Safe?
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM
- **Summary:** The CIR Expert Panel evaluated PEG castor oils and concluded they are safe for use in cosmetics as currently formulated. However, the panel cautioned against formulations that increase penetration, as sensitization cases traced to a moisturizer used on damaged skin. The safety margin runs tightest in baby leave-on products. This is a surfactant/solubilizer used widely in cleansers and serums.
- **Product relevance:** PEG-40 Hydrogenated Castor Oil appears in many of our tracked products. The damaged-skin caveat is directly relevant for SKINgenius users with compromised barriers (eczema, rosacea, post-procedure).
- **Action needed:** Update ingredient profile for PEG-40 Hydrogenated Castor Oil to include "use caution on damaged/compromised skin" note. Consider adding a contraindication flag for active eczema or post-procedure skin.
- **Sources:** HB Mag referencing CIR (Sep 14, 2026) — **Single source; needs CIR primary document**

## Category 3: FDA Cosmetic Labeling / Claims Rules

### Finding 5 — MoCRA Fragrance Allergen Labeling: NPRM Targeted for Late 2026

- **Source:** https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/cosmepedia/us-modernization-of-cosmetics-regulations-act-of-2022-mocra
- **Title:** U.S. Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA)
- **Relevance:** HIGH
- **Summary:** Under the FDA Unified Agenda, the Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM) for Disclosure of Fragrance Allergens in Cosmetic Labeling is **targeted for publication in late 2026**. MoCRA's final fragrance allergen rule will require disclosure of 26 specific allergens on product ingredient lists when present above specified concentration thresholds. EU Regulation 1223/2009 already requires these same 26 allergens. This means the US is moving toward EU-style fragrance allergen labeling but with a multi-year lag.
- **Product relevance:** SKINgenius already tracks fragrance allergens for EU compliance. This confirms the US will follow the same 26-allergen list, meaning our existing allergen-flagging infrastructure will cover both markets with minimal changes.
- **Action needed:** Confirm our ingredient taxonomy already includes all 26 allergens (likely yes, but verify). Prepare to display "contains fragrance allergens" flag in US-facing product pages once the rule is finalized. Monitor FDA Federal Register for the actual NPRM publication.
- **Sources:** ChemLinked (Aug 28, 2026), Care Europe (Sep 13, 2026), Good Housekeeping (Sep 9, 2026) — **3+ independent sources confirmed**

### Finding 6 — MoCRA GMP Rule: Still No Final Rule (Industry Uses ISO 22716)

- **Source:** https://www.orionexregulatory.com/fda-cosmetics-import-guide-2026
- **Title:** MoCRA Compliance Guide for Cosmetics Import 2026
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM
- **Summary:** MoCRA directed FDA to finalize cosmetic GMP regulations by the end of 2025. That deadline was missed; no final rule is in force as of this guide (August 2026). ISO 22716 remains the practical industry reference point. When the final rule arrives, it is expected to align substantially with ISO 22716 but with US-specific deviations.
- **Product relevance:** For SKINgenius's product sourcing and quality scoring, ISO 22716 compliance remains the benchmark. No immediate action needed until the final rule publishes.
- **Action needed:** Monitor for FDA GMP final rule publication. When it arrives, assess whether our product quality scoring needs adjustment.
- **Sources:** Orionex Regulatory (Aug 25, 2026), Care Europe (Sep 13, 2026) — **2+ independent sources confirmed**

### Finding 7 — EU vs US Formula Gap: Sunscreen Filters Most Divergent

- **Source:** https://getskinscore.com/blog/cosmetic-regulation-eu-vs-us-fda-2026/
- **Title:** Cosmetic regulation 2026: EU vs US FDA, what the gap means for you
- **Relevance:** MEDIUM
- **Summary:** The EU has ~1700 banned/restricted ingredients; the US federal ban list is much shorter. The two systems have moved closer in structure (both now require facility registration, product listing, adverse event reporting) but still differ sharply in ingredient-level restrictions. **Sunscreen filter approval is where the two systems diverge most** — the EU approves modern filters (Tinosorb S/M, Uvinul A Plus) that the FDA has not cleared, which is why US and EU versions of the same brand often have different formulas.
- **Product relevance:** SKINgenius's sunscreen recommendations should note when a product contains EU-approved filters not available in the US, or vice versa. This matters for users who travel or purchase cross-border.
- **Action needed:** Add "regional availability" field to sunscreen products in our database. Flag when a recommended sunscreen uses filters not approved in the user's region.
- **Sources:** SkinScore (Sep 10, 2026), ChemLinked (Aug 28, 2026) — **2+ independent sources confirmed**
